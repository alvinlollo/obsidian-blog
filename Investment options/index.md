## **Option 1: Shares**
### What are shares?
Shares are a part ownership of a public company that are sold on the ASX (Australian Stock Exchange). Also referred to as Stocks or equity. When you buy a share of a company you become a shareholder, shareholders are entitled to dividends that represent a company's profit.

### The risk of shares
Shares are high risk but long term. To receive a profit the company you invested in must grow in value and have higher profits from the initial date you purchased the share. This means that you must consider if a company you invest in will grow after you invested in them.

### What can I do to invest safely?
You need to constantly monitor the value of the stock you invest in and make decisions whether to sell or buy. You must not base on how well a company might do just on the graph as you must do the manual work of verifying a company and researching online.

### How do I invest in shares?
You can invest in shares through as stockbroker which takes a fee as a percent of your transaction, when you buy or sell. Stockbrokers also allows you to see the previous value history of any company on the exchange allowing you to determine if a company is stable or growing. Common and verified stockbrokers are: [CMC](https://www.cmcmarkets.com/en-au), [Stake](https://hellostake.com/au), [Interactive Brokers](https://www.interactivebrokers.com.au/en/home.php), and [Commsec](https://www.commsec.com.au/).

### Why should I not invest in shares?
You should not invest in shares if you don't want to constantly monitoring your investment as they can dramatically fall at anytime including when you sleep. Shares are also extremely risky as stock prices fluctuate.

## **Option 2: Exchange-Traded Funds (ETFs) / Index Funds**

### What are ETFs?
Exchange-Traded funds are a pool of money from many investors that are managed by an entity to have a diverse portfolio. ETFs are a hybrid of a managed fund that uses safe strategies such as diversification to spread out shares and investments across multiple different companies and distributes it to investors. ETFs are also managed by professionals that trade all throughout the day so you don't need to monitor the stock market constantly.

### The risk of ETFs
ETFs carry the risk of market volatility, tracking errors, liquidity shortages, and potential closure.

- Market Volatility: The value of an ETF can drop due to the investment made by the ETF manager drops.
- Tracking errors: The tracking stated or given to you may not actually reflect the true performance of the ETF due to fees, expenses and strategies made by the ETF.
- Potential closure: Smaller or unpopular ETFs may shut down where the ETF will try it's best to return all the money accordingly.

### What can I do to invest safely?
Always research the company/people who are managing the ETF, see if they have a good background, how long the ETF has been up and it's reputation. Read the Product Disclosure Statement (PDS) to understand the risks and fees. Only use the top 200 ETFs rather than specific ETFs. Split your money in multiple ETFs and check the ETFs holdings and what assets they own. And take into account the fees of the ETF and the stockbroker.

### How do I invest in ETFs
You can buy and sell units in an ETF through a stockbroker who can show you various different ETFs available. Buys ETFs is the same a buying and selling shares. You can also contact the ETF provider directly to buy and sell funds without using a stockbroker. Well known and genuine stockbrokers that sell ETFs are: [Stake](https://hellostake.com/au), [CMC markets](https://www.cmcmarkets.com/en-au) and [Commsec](https://www.commsec.com.au/).

### Why should I not invest in ETFs?
Since ETFs are very popular for beginners, you cannot outperform the market. If you want to beat the market and have superior gains then you should invest in shares yourself. ETFs are also has many small managing fees that compound overtime which lowers your total returns. And if the ETF you are in are basing their investments in a single area of assets, if that market drops then your money does as well.

## **Option 3: Managed funds**

### What are Managed funds?
Managed funds are a pool of money from many investors to create a diverse portfolio managed by a professional entity. Managed funds are different from ETFs as managed funds are unlisted, priced daily, and don't trade all day. Unlike ETFs, managed funds are not as transparent on their holdings.

### The risk of Managed funds
There is a high risk that the professional manager's investment choices under perform the market leading to loss. Managed funds charge higher management ratios (MER) and performance fees that may reduce your earnings. Unlike shares, it will take several days to withdraw your money as the fund needs to process the request manually, which is time-consuming. And you may be liable for capital gains tax distributions even if you didn't sell your units, due to manager's trading activity.

### What can I do to invest safely?
Always do your due diligence and review the performance history of the fund, check it's 5 - 10 year returns against a relevant bank's benchmark to ensure that they are consistent and have the rate of return you want. Always read the Product Disclosure Statement (PDS) to identify any hidden fees. Diversify your investment, by spreading your investment across multiple funds to mitigate the risk of a single manager making poor decisions. And review the assets the fund is investing in, look at the type of investments that the fund is buying and that it matches your risk tolerance.

### How do I invest in Managed Funds?
You can apply through a fund manager's website by completing an application form and transferring funds. Some managed funds are available via the ASX mFund service allowing you to buy them through brokers such as [Commsec](https://www.commsec.com.au/). You can also provide access to wholesale managed funds, allowing you to access to wholesale managed funds that often have lower fees than retail versions. Some well known and genuine managed funds are: [Vanguard Australia](https://www.vanguard.com.au/personal/), [BlackRock](https://www.blackrock.com/au), and [BetaShares](https://www.betashares.com.au/).

### Why should I not invest in Managed Funds?
You should not invest in managed funds if you are concerned about costs, the higher management fees often make managed funds more expensive than low -cost ETFs over long periods of time. Lack of control, you cannot choose the investments that the manger makes, meaning you may hold stocks for companies you may not support. Under performance, many active managers fail to beat the market index after the fees are accounted for.
